Concept 1 · Current shell, new contents

Owner's Five

Identical chrome to today's Lucille — same sidebar, header, greeting, card language. The change is what's ON it: the 20-item flat sidebar collapses into the five things an owner actually thinks in — Money · People · Metrics · Operations · Lucille. Every dashboard card is one of those domains with its headline number and 3 drill links. Zero retraining cost.

Concept 3 · Original — attention-first

Pulse

Flips the model: instead of you hunting for information, Lucille triages the business and puts "What needs you" front and center, ranked by money-at-stake. Domains become lenses (tabs), not folders. A slim icon rail replaces the sidebar; a right rail keeps live tickers and — key to the organic feel — a Related trail that follows your attention across domains the way your brain actually hops.

Concept 4 · Original — command-first bento

Canvas

No sidebar at all. One command bar is the whole navigation — type, talk, or hit ⌘K from anywhere — over a bento canvas where every card is itself conversational: hover any tile and "Ask Lucille about this" appears in place. Cards resize to what the client's stack actually feeds (plugin/API-aware): rich stack → dense canvas; two integrations → four big tiles. The layout IS the onboarding meter.

Why it works
One navigation habit total: the bar. Nothing to memorize, no tree to learn — search-first is how this generation already navigates (Spotlight, ChatGPT, Raycast). Cards carry Related chips, so browsing is associative, not hierarchical.
Stack-aware by design
The canvas is composed from whatever the client's integrations feed — the bottom strip literally sells the next connection ("+ QuickBooks unlocks a P&L tile"). Great demo moment: dashboard grows as the stack connects.
Trade-off
Boldest bet: some owners want a visible menu for confidence. Discoverability rests entirely on ⌘K affordance — mitigate with the Related chips and a "Browse all" command.
Concept 5 · Jarvis / Albert galaxy UI — Lucille as the brain, side menu kept

Constellation

The Albert 3D-brain look, worn by Lucille: the business is a living galaxy of floating nodes on a night-dawn canvas — Lucille's sun at the center, the four domains orbiting, sub-nodes linked the way the business actually connects (Marcus ties to Money through commissions, not just to "Team"). Click a node to fly to it; the HUD pill at the bottom is talk-or-type, exactly like Albert. Same Dawn colors — ember, honey, iris, rose — just after dark. The current sidebar stays.

Why it works
Pure spatial memory — the strongest human recall system. Money always lives upper-left, People upper-right; after a week the owner navigates without reading. The cross-links (Marcus ↔ Commissions) make the "brain-like" non-rigid navigation literal and visible.
Branding note
Same Dawn tokens on a night canvas derived from --text (#1B1216 family) — ember/honey/iris/rose glow exactly as-is. Read it as "Dawn, before sunrise." Sidebar unchanged, dark-skinned. If a dark mode is off-brand for launch, this ships as a toggleable "Galaxy view" next to Dashboard.
Trade-off
Highest wow-per-demo of all five — and the least conventional daily driver. Numbers-dense work (commission tables, funnels) still needs the classic pages behind each node. Best positioned as the signature view that sells the "your business has a brain now" story.
